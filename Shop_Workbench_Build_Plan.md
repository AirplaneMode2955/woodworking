# Shop Workbench — Build Plan

**Overall size:** 48" long x 16" deep x 34" tall
**Top:** knotty pine/spruce-fir edge-glued panel (laminated for strength)
**Second level:** OSB tool shelf, ~8" off the floor

**Note:** the project panel is stain-grade pine/spruce-fir, not hardwood — it'll look clean and finish well, but it's softer than oak/maple and will dent under hard shop use. If you want true hardwood durability later, swap it for a 3/4" oak or maple panel of the same size and everything below still applies.

## Design

The top is only 16" deep as-is, so I designed the whole frame around that footprint (legs at all 4 corners, apron running the full perimeter). To add strength without buying a thicker top, the project panel gets glued and screwed to an OSB backer cut to match — that doubles top thickness to ~1.5" and the OSB never shows since it's screwed up from underneath.

Confirm actual OSB thickness before cutting legs (most is either 7/16" or 23/32"). Leg length below assumes a combined top thickness of 1.5". If yours measures differently: **leg length = 34" minus your actual top+backer thickness.**

## Tools needed

Circular saw, drill/driver, clamps, tape measure, speed square, sandpaper or orbital sander. No table saw or miter saw required — everything below is straight cuts and butt joints.

## Step-by-step

1. **Cut all 2x4 pieces** per the board map above. Square each end with the speed square before cutting.
2. **Build the top frame (apron):** Lay the 2 long rails (45") and 2 short rails (13") around the 4 legs to form a rectangle at the top, legs flush to the outside corners. Pre-drill 3/16" pilot holes through the rail ends into the leg faces, glue the joint, then drive two 3" screws per joint.
3. **Build the lower stretcher frame** the same way, using the 2 long stretchers (45") and 2 short stretchers (13"), positioned 8" up from the bottom of the legs.
4. **Add the center brace:** screw the 13" center brace across the middle of the top frame (running depth-wise), flush with the top edge of the long rails — this keeps the benchtop from flexing under pressure in the middle.
5. **Add corner gussets:** cut 4 OSB triangles and screw each one into the inside corner of the top frame (into both the leg and the rail) — this is what actually stops the bench from racking side to side. Two screws per side, per gusset.
6. **Laminate the top:** spread wood glue on the OSB backer, clamp it to the underside of the project panel, then drive 1-1/4" screws up through the OSB into the panel on an 8" grid (screw heads stay hidden underneath). Let it clamp/cure at least an hour before handling.
7. **Attach the top to the frame:** set the laminated top on the apron frame, centered, and screw up through the top rails into the top assembly (4-6 screws, avoid the very center where it'd show through the panel top).
8. **Cut cleats** from your board scrap — four ~10" strips of leftover 2x4 (or thin scrap), screw one to the inside face of each lower stretcher, about 3/4" down from the top edge, so the shelf can rest on them.
9. **Drop in the tool shelf:** set the OSB shelf piece on the four cleats. No fasteners needed — it lifts out for cleaning or swapping totes, but you can screw it down if you want it fixed.
10. **Sand everything:** 120 grit first pass, 220 to finish, focus on the top and any edges you'll be handling.
11. **Finish the top:** wipe on Danish oil or poly, 2 coats, light sanding between coats. Leave the frame and shelf raw — no need to finish structural lumber on a shop bench.
12. **Cure time:** let the top finish dry a full 24 hours before loading tools or clamping work to it.

## Difficulty & time

Beginner-to-intermediate. Straight cuts, butt joints, no advanced joinery. Budget **4-6 hours of shop time** across cutting, assembly, and glue-up, plus overnight cure on the laminated top and 24 hours on the finish before use.
