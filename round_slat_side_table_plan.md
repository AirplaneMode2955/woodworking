# Round Slatted Side Table — Build Plan

Matches the reference photo: round slatted top, chamfered edge, four splayed tapered legs, warm natural wood tone. Sized as a drink table next to a chair.

**Finished size:** 20" diameter top x 20" tall
**Difficulty:** Moderate
**Build time:** ~1 day (plus overnight glue-up and finish dry time)

## Tools Needed
- Circular saw
- Jigsaw (needed for the round top cutout — if you don't already have one, they run ~$40-60 new)
- Drill/driver
- Clamps (at least 4-6 bar or pipe clamps for the top glue-up)
- Orbital sander + 120/220 grit sandpaper
- Tape measure, square, pencil, straightedge
- String + pencil or a scrap-wood trammel (for marking the circle)

## Shopping List
- 2x 1x4x8' poplar or select pine boards (tabletop slats)
- 1x 2x2x6' hardwood (poplar, oak, or similar — tapered legs)
- 1x 1x4x4' pine (scrap-grade fine — underside cleats)
- 4x angled leg mounting plates (hairpin-style leg brackets or standard angled leg plates, sold in sets of 4)
- 1.25" wood screws (coarse thread)
- Wood glue
- 120 and 220 grit sandpaper
- Wipe-on poly or Danish/teak oil (for the warm tone in the photo)

## Cut List

| Part | Qty | Dimensions | Cut From |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top slats | 6 | 3.5" x 22" x 0.75" | 2x 1x4x8' boards (3 slats per board) |
| Underside cleats | 2 | 2.5" x 15" x 0.75" | 1x4x4' scrap |
| Legs | 4 | 1.75" tapering to 1" x 17" long | 2x2x6' hardwood (cut to 17" first, then taper) |

## Step-by-Step

**1. Build the tabletop blank**
Lay the 6 slats face-down, edges touching, with 1/8" spacers (a nickel works) between each. Place the two cleats perpendicular across the slats, one 3" from each end, square to the slats. Glue and screw each cleat to each slat (one 1.25" screw per slat/cleat intersection, pilot-drilled so you don't split the wood). Clamp the whole assembly flat while the glue sets — let it sit at least 4 hours, ideally overnight.

**2. Mark and cut the circle**
Find the center of the glued-up blank. Tie a pencil to a string exactly 10" from a center pin (or use a scrap-wood trammel arm), and swing a 20"-diameter circle. Cut just outside the line with the jigsaw, then sand to the line with the orbital sander.

**3. Chamfer the top edge**
Set your sander at an angle and hand-sand a consistent bevel around the top edge of the circle (about 1/8"-3/16" wide) to match the softened edge in the photo. Go slow — consistency matters more than depth.

**4. Taper the legs**
On each leg blank, mark a taper from 1.75" square at the top to 1" x 1" at the bottom on the two inside-facing sides only (the two outside faces stay straight, matching the photo's look). Clamp a straightedge guide to the leg as a fence and run the circular saw down each taper line. Clean up the cut faces with the sander.

**5. Mount the leg plates**
Attach one angled leg mounting plate to the underside of each leg top. Position the four plates on the underside cleats/tabletop so the legs splay outward evenly (roughly 10-12° from vertical) and are centered under the circle. Screw the plates into the cleats and directly into the tabletop slats for extra hold.

**6. Sand everything**
Full sand pass at 120 grit, then 220 grit, over the whole table — top, edge, legs. Wipe off dust with a damp cloth.

**7. Finish**
Apply 2-3 coats of wipe-on poly or Danish/teak oil, following the can's dry time between coats, to get the warm honey-teak tone shown in the photo.

**8. Let cure**
Give the finish 24-48 hours to fully cure before setting drinks on it.
