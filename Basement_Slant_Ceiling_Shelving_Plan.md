# Basement Slant-Ceiling Storage Shelf — Build Plan (v2: follows the ceiling line)

**Space:** ~5ft wide x 1ft deep nook, ceiling slants from ~4ft (short end) to ~7ft (tall end)
**Unit:** Freestanding — a flat-top box on the bottom, with an angled-top "topper" stacked on the tall end that follows the ceiling slope, matching your sketch.
**Use:** Bulk paper towels, toilet paper, pantry overflow — open shelving, cold-storage style

Updated from the flat-top version now that you can get something that does angled cuts — this version chases the extra height on the tall side instead of leaving it empty.

**Before you cut:** your dimensions were estimates. On site, check with a speed square or angle finder against the actual ceiling and confirm the drop really is close to 36" of height over the 60" width (that's what gives the 31° angle below). If it's noticeably different, the whole topper's post heights and angle scale with it — flag it and we'll adjust the numbers.

---

## Design

Two stacked sections:

**1. Lower box** — full 60" width, flat top, 46" tall (2" clearance under a true 4ft/48" low point). Same flat-shelf construction the whole way across: 5 legs (four corners + one center-back support so the 60" span doesn't sag), cleats, plywood shelves. Gives you 3 shelf levels / 2 storage bays across the entire width.

**2. Upper "topper"** — sits on top of the box, but only over the tall 42" of the width (past that point the ceiling's already down near box height, not worth framing). Posts step up in height to match the slope, their tops cut at a consistent 31° angle, and a plywood panel rests flat across those angled tops — that panel IS the sloped roofline, sitting flush under your actual ceiling. One partial shelf inside the topper, on the tall end, sized to where there's still enough headroom to be useful.

**The angle:** rise 36" over a 60" run = **31° off horizontal** (some saws/gauges read angle off vertical instead — that's the complement, 59°). This is a single flat-plane angle, not a compound miter — every cut in this plan uses the same 31° setting.

| Piece | Qty | Material | Dimensions | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tall posts | 2 | 2x4 | 36" long, top cut at 31° | Front & back, at the tall (0") end of the topper |
| Mid posts | 2 | 2x4 | 20" long, top cut square | Front & back, 27" in from the tall end — supports the far edge of the topper shelf |
| Short posts | 2 | 2x4 | 11" long, top cut at 31° | Front & back, 42" in from the tall end — where the roofline meets the box top |
| Shelf-support cleats | 4 | 2x4 | 3.5" long | Screwed to inside face of the 2 tall posts, at 20" up, to catch the near edge of the topper shelf |
| Topper shelf | 1 | 3/4" plywood | 27" x 12" | Square-cut both ends — sits on the tall-post cleats and rests on top of the mid posts |
| Roof panel | 1 | 3/4" plywood | 49" x 12" | Square rectangle — no bevel needed on the panel itself, it lies flat across the angled post tops and that tilt becomes the slope |

Why 49" for the roof panel and not 42": that's the actual sloped distance (hypotenuse) between the tall posts and short posts, not the flat horizontal run — cut it long enough to fully close the top.

**Cutting the angled post tops:** set your saw/miter tool to 31° and cut the SAME direction on all 4 angled posts (2 tall + 2 short) so they all lean the same way — lay them side by side before cutting to double check the angle faces the same direction on all of them. The mid posts stay square-cut; they only carry the shelf, not the roof panel.

## Plywood Layout (fits on ONE 4x8 sheet — cut in this order to avoid waste)

Rip the sheet into four 12"-wide strips (full 96" length) with a straightedge/track guide, then crosscut each strip like this:

- **Strip 1:** cut a 60" box shelf, then from the 36" remainder cut the 27" topper shelf (9" scrap left over).
- **Strip 2:** cut a 60" box shelf (36" scrap).
- **Strip 3:** cut a 60" box shelf (36" scrap).
- **Strip 4:** cut the 49" roof panel (47" scrap left over — big enough to save for a small future project).

That's all 6 topper/box plywood pieces from a single sheet.

---

## Wood Recommendation

**Framing (legs + posts + cleats): construction-grade SPF 2x4 studs** — cheapest option, cuts and bevels easily with a basic saw, plenty strong for this load.

**Shelf/roof panels: 3/4" sanded pine (BC) plywood** — smoother than CDX for a few dollars more, and since the roof panel and topper shelf edges will be visible on the tall end, worth the step up. CDX still works fine structurally if you want to shave the cost.

No stain or finish needed — bare wood is standard for basement storage. Skip it unless you want the look.

## Step-by-Step

**Box (build first, same as a standard flat shelf):**
1. Verify the box side of the space clears with the actual ceiling low point. Adjust the 46" target if your measurement is off.
2. Cut 5 legs to 45.25" (2x4x8' studs, 2 per stud).
3. Mark cleat heights on one leg (2.25", 16.75", 31.25", 45.25"), clamp all 5 bottoms-flush, transfer marks across all of them with a speed square.
4. Cut 20 cleats at 3.5" with a stop block.
5. Glue + screw cleats to the inside face of each leg at its marked heights.
6. Stand the 4 corner legs at the footprint corners (60"x12") plus the center-back leg at the 30" mark.
7. Cut the plywood per the layout above.
8. Screw down the bottom shelf first, check the frame is square (diagonal measurements should match), then work up through the remaining 3 shelves.

**Topper (build on top of the box, tall end):**
9. Cut the 2 tall posts (36"), 2 mid posts (20"), 2 short posts (11"). Set your angle tool to 31° and cut the tops of the 2 tall + 2 short posts (mid posts stay square).
10. Stand the tall posts at the very tall end of the box top (front and back, flush with the box's tall-end corners), the mid posts 27" in from there, and the short posts 42" in — screw all 6 down into the box top/legs below.
11. Screw the 4 shelf-support cleats to the inside faces of the 2 tall posts at 20" up.
12. Set the 27"x12" topper shelf on the tall-post cleats and the mid-post tops, screw down.
13. Lay the 49"x12" roof panel across the angled tops of the tall and short posts — it should sit flat, tilted at the slope. Screw down into all 4 post tops.
14. Sand all exposed plywood edges (120 grit).
15. Move the whole unit into the nook and check the roof panel actually sits parallel to your real ceiling — a gap of an inch or so is fine and expected (that's your clearance margin), but if it's way off, that's your cue the angle or a height needs a field adjustment before you call it done.

---

## Difficulty & Time

**Difficulty:** Beginner-Intermediate — the box is straightforward butt-joint work; the topper adds one repeated angled cut (31°, same setting for all 4 posts) and a bit more layout care.
**Time:** A full day. Roughly 1.5 hrs cutting box lumber + plywood, 1 hr cutting topper posts/angles, 2.5–3 hrs assembly (box first, then topper), plus a final fit-check once it's in place.
