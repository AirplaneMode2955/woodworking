# Walnut Floating Shelf — Build Plan

**Project:** 4' floating shelf, solid walnut, for office
**Finished size:** 48"L x 7.25"D x 1.5"T
**Difficulty:** Intermediate (glue-up + one bevel rip)
**Active shop time:** ~4-5 hours, spread over 2-3 days (glue and finish need cure time)

## Design notes

Floating shelves live or die on the mount, not the shelf itself. With no table saw, the two common approaches are (a) buy a hidden steel bracket kit, or (b) build a French cleat. Going with the **French cleat** — it's fully buildable with a circular saw (bevel set to 45°), it's free once you've got a scrap 1x4, and it's the sturdiest option for a 4-footer since the cleat spans multiple wall studs instead of relying on two bracket points.

Shelf itself is a 2-layer glue-up of 3/4" walnut to get a full 1.5" thick face — reads as a substantial solid slab instead of a thin board, and doesn't need any lumber thicker than what's sold at a home center.

The two cleat pieces come from **one** 1x4 x 4' board, ripped once lengthwise at 45°. No waste beyond the saw kerf.

## Wood recommendation

**Walnut project panels** (pre-glued hardwood panels, sold at Home Depot/Lowe's in the hardwood/hobby lumber section) for the shelf — walnut's the right call for an office piece: dark, tight grain, takes oil finish beautifully, and doesn't need a table saw to look finished since the panels come pre-surfaced and edge-glued to width. Don't bother trying to source rough walnut and dimension it yourself without a jointer/planer — the pre-glued panels solve that problem for you.

**Pine 1x4** for the cleat — it's hidden entirely once mounted, so no reason to spend walnut money on it.

## Tools needed

- Circular saw + straightedge/track guide (square the panel ends, bevel-rip the cleat)
- Clamped stop block (repeatable length cuts if trimming both panels to match)
- Router with 3/4" straight bit (rabbet for the cleat) and roundover bit (front-edge finish, optional)
- Drill/driver, with a countersink/pilot bit
- Palm sander
- Clamps (as many as you've got — bar or pipe clamps ideal, at least 4-6 for the glue-up)
- Wood glue
- Level (for mounting the wall cleat)
- Stud finder

## Step-by-step

**1. Square and match the panels**
Set your straightedge and trim both walnut panels to exactly 48" long, using a clamped stop block so both cuts land at the identical length. Check the panels are the same width — if one runs wider, rip it down to match with the straightedge.

**2. Glue up the shelf slab**
Spread glue evenly across one full face of a panel, stack the second panel on top face-to-face, and clamp every 6-8" along the length — alternate clamps top and bottom of the stack to keep even pressure and prevent bowing. Wipe squeeze-out immediately. Let cure a full 24 hours before unclamping.

**3. Clean up the glued slab**
Once cured, run the circular saw + straightedge along both long edges to true them up (glue-ups rarely stay perfectly aligned face-to-face). Check the ends are still square; if not, re-trim with the stop block setup from step 1.

**4. Rout the cleat rabbet**
On the underside of the shelf, 1" in from the back edge, rout a 3/4" wide x 3/8" deep rabbet running the full 48" length. This is where the shelf-side cleat piece will sit flush — set the router to a straightedge guide clamped square to the shelf, take it in two passes (don't try to hog the full 3/8" depth in one pass).

**5. Round over the front edge (optional but recommended)**
Swap to the roundover bit and ease the front edge and two visible ends of the shelf. Skip the back edge and underside — nobody sees them.

**6. Sand**
120 grit first pass across the top, front edge, and ends, then 220 to finish. Hand-sand the roundover and rabbet corners where the palm sander can't reach.

**7. Build the cleat**
Set your circular saw's bevel to 45° and rip the pine 1x4 in half lengthwise, guided by the straightedge. You'll get two mirror-image angled strips — one is the wall side, one is the shelf side.

**8. Mount the shelf-side cleat**
Glue and screw the shelf-side cleat strip into the rabbet from step 4, angled face pointing down and out (toward the front of the shelf), flush with the shelf's back edge. Pilot-drill before screwing to avoid splitting the pine.

**9. Finish the shelf**
Apply Danish oil or walnut oil in thin coats with a rag, following the grain. Let the first coat sit per the product's working time, wipe off the excess, and let cure per label (usually 6-8 hours) before the second coat. Don't skip the wipe-off — walnut blotches if oil is left to pool.

**10. Mount the wall cleat**
Find studs with the stud finder and mark them. Hold the wall-side cleat strip level against the wall, angled face pointing up and out (mating orientation to the shelf-side piece), and screw it into every stud it crosses with the 3" structural screws — minimum two studs, three if your stud spacing allows across the 46" length.

**11. Hang the shelf**
Lift the shelf and hook the shelf-side cleat over the wall-side cleat, sliding down until the angled faces lock together and the shelf sits flush against the wall. No visible fasteners, no visible cleat — just a floating slab of walnut.
