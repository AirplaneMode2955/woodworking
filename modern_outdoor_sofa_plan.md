# Modern Chunky-Leg Outdoor Sofa — Build Plan (2x4 / 2x6 only)

Matches the reference photos: chunky laminated legs, wide flat armrests, slatted seat and back, stained a warm honey/chestnut tone. Built entirely from 2x4 and 2x6 stock — no 4x4 needed. The "chunky post" look comes from gluing and screwing two 2x4s face-to-face, which gives a solid ~3" x 3.5" post, close to a 4x4's footprint, straighter stock, and no dado/notch cutting required. Sized as a 3-seat couch (84" wide). The matching single chair is next — same joinery, scaled down.

**Difficulty:** Intermediate. Laminating the legs takes patience (everything has to stay flush while clamped), but every joint in this build is a straight cut and a butt joint — no notching, no dados.
**Build time:** 8-10 hours over a weekend (cutting, laminating, assembly, sanding, finish coats with dry time between).
**Tools needed:** circular saw, drill/driver, clamps (at least 6-8 for the leg lamination step), sander, tape measure/square. No table saw, dado blade, or chisel work required.

## How the Legs Work

Each leg is two pieces of 2x4 glued and screwed face-to-face along their full length, creating one solid post (~3" x 3.5" actual). No notching — the seat frame simply butts against the inside face of the leg and gets screwed through. The armrest sits flat on top of the front leg and extends back to the top of the rear leg.

## Wood Recommendation

**Primary: Western Red Cedar.** This is seating furniture — cedar has no chemical treatment to worry about, it's lighter to work with on a circular saw, and it takes the honey/chestnut stain in your reference photos better than pressure-treated pine (PT has a greenish cast that fights warm stain colors for the first year).

**Budget alternative: Pressure-treated pine.** Runs roughly $110-130 less on this build. Same tools, just heavier, and it needs a few weeks to dry out before stain/sealant will take.

## Build Instructions

### 1. Cut everything to length first
Cut all leg plies, rails, slats, and armrests to the dimensions in the cut list. Label each piece with painter's tape as you go.

### 2. Laminate the legs
For each leg, spread exterior wood glue on one face of a 2x4 ply, clamp the matching second ply on top making sure both ends and edges are flush, then drive 3" screws through both plies every 8" along the length (stagger them so you're not screwing into a previous screw). Let clamps sit at least 30 minutes before moving on. Do all 6 legs (front x2, back x2, center front x1, center back x1) this way.

### 3. Build the seat frame
Glue and screw the front rail to the inside face of both front legs at the 17.5" mark from the bottom (2x 3" screws per joint, pilot-drilled to avoid splitting). Attach the back rail to the back legs the same way. Add the two side rails connecting front to back, and the center stretcher between the center front and center back legs. Square the whole frame with a framing square before the glue sets — check that both diagonals measure the same.

### 4. Confirm the back legs are square to the frame
With the back legs already screwed into the back rail from step 3, screw the center back leg into the back rail and center stretcher for support against the long back slat span.

### 5. Lay the seat slats
Starting from one side, screw down the 14 seat slats (2x 2.5" screws per end, into the front and back rails), keeping a consistent 1/4" gap between each — a couple of stacked nickels make a quick spacer jig. Pre-drill near the ends to prevent splitting.

### 6. Attach the back slats
Starting at the bottom (right above the seat slats) and working up, screw the 5 horizontal back slats into the back legs and center back leg, keeping a consistent 1.5" gap between rows. Use a scrap block as a spacer gauge.

### 7. Mount the armrests
Set each armrest flat on top of its front leg, extending back to rest on top of the rear leg. Screw down from the top (2 screws over the front leg, 2 over the rear leg), then drill and glue in wood plugs over the screw heads, sand flush once dry.

### 8. Sand
Work through 80, then 120, then 220 grit on all surfaces and edges. Ease every sharp corner slightly — this matters more outdoors since bare skin and swimsuits will be against it.

### 9. Finish
Apply stain per the can's instructions (wipe on, let sit, wipe off excess), let dry fully, then apply 2 coats of exterior sealant with light sanding (220 grit) between coats.
