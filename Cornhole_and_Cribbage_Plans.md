# Cornhole Boards + Cribbage Board — Build Plans

Basic power tools assumed throughout: circular saw, drill/driver, jigsaw or hole saw, orbital/hand sander, clamps, tape measure, speed square. No table saw, miter saw, or router required. Full shopping list — nothing assumed on hand.

---

## Project 1: Cornhole Boards (Set of 2, Regulation Size)

**Dimensions:** 24" W x 48" L playing surface per board, 6" scoring hole centered 9" from the top edge and 12" from each side. Legs fold out at the hole end to raise it to 12" high.

### Cut List

| Piece | Qty | Dimensions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plywood top | 2 | 24" x 48" x 1/2" | One 4'x8' sheet, ripped in half — zero waste |
| Long frame rail | 4 | 48" x 3.5" (2x4 on edge) | 2 boards @ 8' (2 rails per board) |
| Short frame rail | 4 | 21" x 3.5" | 1 board @ 8' (4 pieces per board) |
| Leg | 4 | 14" x 3.5" | 1 board @ 8' (plenty of extra) |
| Center brace (recommended) | 2 | 21" x 3.5" | Cut from the same 8' board as short rails, size up to a 2nd board if it runs short |

### Wood Recommendation
**Primary: pine 2x4 studs + 1/2" BC sanded plywood.** Cheapest option, cuts and drills easily with a circular saw and hand tools, and gets fully covered by primer/paint anyway so appearance of the raw wood doesn't matter. If you'd rather skip painting and go with a clear/stained look, **cedar 2x4s + cedar-faced plywood** is the upgrade — costs more but you skip repainting every couple seasons if these live outside.

### Shopping List
- 1 sheet 1/2" BC sanded plywood, 4'x8'
- 4x 2x4x8' pine studs
- 4x 3" exterior utility hinges
- 4x hook-and-eye leg locks (or short chain sets)
- Box of 2.5" exterior wood screws
- Box of 1.25" exterior wood screws

- Exterior polyurethane, clear
- Sandpaper: 100, 150, 220 grit
- 6" hole saw (or use a jigsaw + compass-drawn circle if you'd rather not buy one)

### Build Steps
1. Rip the plywood sheet into two 24" x 48" pieces with the circular saw and a straightedge guide.
2. Cut the 2x4s: 4 long rails @ 48", 4 short rails @ 21", 4 legs @ 14", 2 center braces @ 21".
3. On each plywood top, mark the hole center: 9" in from one 48" edge (this is the "top"/far edge), 12" from each side. Drill a starter hole and cut the 6" circle with a hole saw, or a jigsaw following a compass line.
4. Build each frame: stand 2 long rails on edge, 24" apart outer-to-outer. Fit the 2 short rails between them at each end, flush to the outside. Predrill, glue, and screw each corner (2x 2.5" screws per joint).
5. Check the frame is square by measuring both diagonals — adjust until they match, then clamp until the glue sets.
6. Screw the center brace across the frame's midpoint for extra rigidity against warping.
7. Set the plywood top on the frame (hole at the far end), flush on all sides. Predrill and screw every 6-8" around the perimeter and into the center brace, using 1.25" screws.
8. Mount a leg at each corner of the far (hole) end, about 3" in from the edge, with a hinge so it folds flat for transport and swings out to prop that end to 12" high. Attach the leg lock to hold it open.
9. Sand the whole board, 100 → 150 → 220 grit, rounding over sharp edges and the hole rim.
10. Prime, let dry, paint the base color, let dry, tape off and paint the design/logo, remove tape once dry.
11. Apply 2-3 coats of exterior polyurethane, light 320-grit sanding between coats.
12. Repeat for board two. Let cure fully (24-48 hrs) before play.

**Difficulty:** Moderate — squaring the frame and cutting a clean circular hole by hand are the two spots that take patience.
**Time:** One weekend (6-8 hours build time) plus drying/cure time between finish coats.

## Project 2: Cribbage Board

**Dimensions:** 15" L x 3.5" W x 3/4" thick hardwood, single board — no glue-up required.

### Cut List

| Piece | Qty | Dimensions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Board body | 1 | 15" x 3.5" x 3/4" | 1x4 hardwood, buy a 3' length for margin |

### Wood Recommendation
**Primary: walnut.** Dark, classic cribbage-board look, hard enough that peg holes stay crisp over years of use, and a 1x4x3' board is a manageable cost for a project this small. **Budget alternative: maple** — lighter color, harder than walnut so it drills and wears just as well, and typically $5-10 cheaper for the same length. Both are fine with hand tools; you're only making one crosscut, no ripping.

### Shopping List
- 1x4 walnut (or maple) board, 3' length, actual size 3/4" x 3.5"
- 1/4" straight (non-tapered) plug cutter, for making your own wood pegs
- Contrasting scrap hardwood at least 1" thick for the pegs (maple or cherry offcut if the board is walnut, or vice versa — makes the two players' pegs easy to tell apart)
- Danish oil or wipe-on tung oil (food-safe finish not required since it's not a cutting surface, but a hand-friendly oil finish is standard)
- Sandpaper: 150, 220, 320 grit
- Printed cribbage board hole-spacing template (free printable, 1:1 scale — search "printable cribbage board template," print at 100% and verify against a ruler before use)
- Painter's tape (to hold the template in place)

Swap the board to maple and this comes down a few dollars. If you already own a plug cutter set, drop that $12 and you're right back around $38. Prices are estimates — check against Farr West/Ogden hardwood pricing, since specialty hardwoods vary more store to store than construction lumber.

### Build Steps
1. Crosscut the 1x4 to 15" length with the circular saw, using a clamped straightedge for a clean square cut.
2. Sand all faces and edges, 150 then 220 grit, before drilling — much easier to get flat now than around finished holes.
3. Print the cribbage hole template at 100% scale, double-check it against a ruler, then tape it securely and centered onto the board face.
4. Drill the board's scoring holes with a **1/4" bit** (matched to the plug-cutter pegs below) — test the fit on a scrap offcut first, and step down to 15/64" if the pegs come out feeling loose. Wrap a piece of tape around the bit 1/4" from the tip as a depth stop so every hole comes out the same depth.
5. Drill every hole marked on the template, keeping the bit vertical — prop a speed square next to it as a visual reference if you don't have a drill press.
6. Drill the 5 small "games won" tracking holes off to one side, same bit and depth.
7. On the underside or one end, drill 1-2 peg-storage holes about 3/8" diameter (roomier than the scoring holes since these just need to hold the pegs loosely, not friction-fit them) and 3/4" deep, sized to fit all 4 pegs.
8. Peel off the template, sand away any paper fuzz or tear-out around the holes with fine sandpaper.
9. Round over all edges and corners for a comfortable hand-feel.
10. **Make the pegs:** using the 1/4" plug cutter chucked in the drill, bore straight down into a piece of contrasting hardwood scrap at least 1" thick, going as deep as the cutter allows. Pop the plugs free with a flathead screwdriver or by prying from the side. Cut 6-8 plugs so you've got spares. Trim each to about 3/4"-1" long, and sand a slight round or taper onto one tip so it seats into the board without forcing.
11. Wipe on 2-3 coats of Danish or tung oil on both the board and the pegs, wiping excess after each coat, light 320-grit sanding between coats.
12. Insert pegs and check the fit — snug enough that they don't fall out on their own, but not so tight you have to pry them out.

**Difficulty:** Beginner-to-moderate — the woodworking is simple; a steady hand (or the printed template) for consistent hole spacing is what separates a good result from a sloppy one. Making the pegs adds one extra step but no extra difficulty.
**Time:** 3-4 hours build time, plus 24+ hours between oil coats.
