# Desk Hat Rack — Build Plan
*Wall-mounted, 1/2" plywood + dowel pegs, holds 5-6 hats*

## Design
Panel: **30"W x 22"H x 1/2" plywood** (scrap on hand). Fits comfortably in your 3'-wide desk-wall space without filling all 6' of height — this keeps it low and doesn't crowd the wall above it. If your scrap piece is smaller than 30x22, trim the design down proportionally and space the pegs a bit closer together; the layout math below scales fine.

6 pegs in a **zigzag row** (alternating heights) rather than one straight line — looks more intentional and keeps hat crowns from bumping into each other when hung side by side.

| Peg # | X (from left edge) | Y (from top edge) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3" | 7" |
| 2 | 7.75" | 11" |
| 3 | 12.5" | 7" |
| 4 | 17.25" | 11" |
| 5 | 22" | 7" |
| 6 | 26.75" | 11" |

Mount the panel so its bottom edge sits at least 10-12" above your desk surface — gives caps room to hang without their brims resting on the desk.

## Cut List
| Piece | Qty | Dimensions | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| Back panel | 1 | 30" x 22" x 1/2" | Your plywood scrap |
| Hat pegs | 6 | 1" dia. x 4" long | Oak dowel rod (buy) |
| French cleat (wall side) | 1 | ~24" x 2", 45° bevel | 1x2 pine or plywood offcut |

## Tools Needed
Circular saw + straightedge, drill/driver with a 1" spade or Forstner bit + countersink bit, router with a 1/4" roundover bit, palm sander, clamps, tape measure, speed square, a stud finder, and a miter box + hand saw for cutting the dowel pegs to even length.

## Wood Recommendation
**1" diameter oak dowel rod** for the pegs — it's the standard size at Home Depot/Lowe's, workable with a hand drill, and holds up fine to repeated hat removal/placement without splitting. Cheaper alternative: poplar dowel, a couple bucks less, slightly softer — fine if you're painting the pegs instead of leaving them natural. For the cleat strips, a 1x2 pine board is easiest; use leftover plywood instead if you have enough scrap and want to skip that purchase entirely.

## Shopping List
- 1" x 36" (or 48") oak hardwood dowel rod — 1 piece (yields 6 pegs w/ extra)
- 1x2 pine, ~3 ft — for the French cleat (skip if using plywood scrap instead)
- Wood screws (panel-to-cleat, cleat-to-wall) — likely already on hand
- Drywall toggle anchors — only if the wall cleat can't land on a stud anywhere along its length
- Wood glue, sandpaper (120/220), paint or clear coat — already on hand, not priced below

## Step-by-Step
1. **Cut the panel** to 30" x 22" (or your scrap's max size) with the circular saw guided by a straightedge. Square all four edges.
2. **Lay out the peg centers** using the table above — measure and mark each X/Y point with a pencil and speed square. Double-check spacing before drilling.
3. **Drill the peg holes** with the 1" spade/Forstner bit, straight through the panel at each mark. Back the panel with a scrap board underneath to prevent blowout on the exit side.
4. **Cut 6 dowel pegs** to 4" each from the oak rod using the miter box + hand saw for consistent, even lengths.
5. **Dry-fit** each dowel in its hole. If a hole's loose, wrap a strip of tape around the dowel end for a snugger glue fit.
6. **Glue and install pegs**: glue inside each hole and on the dowel end, insert from the front so ~3.5" projects out front, flush (or just under) on the back. Wipe excess glue and let cure overnight before handling.
7. **Sand** the panel faces (120 then 220 grit, palm sander) and hand-sand the edges and peg holes.
8. **Router roundover**: run a 1/4" roundover bit around the front-facing perimeter edges only (skip the back, it's against the wall) for a finished look.
9. **Finish**: paint or clear-coat the panel; finish the pegs separately (stain, oil, or clear coat) if you want a natural-wood-against-painted-panel contrast look. Let dry fully between coats.
10. **Build the French cleat**: cut two ~24" strips from your 1x2 (or plywood scrap), bevel one long edge of each at 45° with the circular saw. Screw one strip to the back of the panel near the top, bevel edge up/out. Screw the mating strip to the wall at your marked, level mounting height, bevel facing down/out so the two interlock.
11. **Find studs** with a stud finder along your mounting line. Screw the wall cleat into at least one stud where possible; use toggle anchors anywhere it lands on bare drywall.
12. **Hang the panel** on the wall cleat, check level, load it up.

## Difficulty & Time
Easy-moderate. Active build time: ~2 hours (cutting, drilling, gluing). Add overnight for glue cure before final sanding/finish, and a day or two if you're doing multiple paint/finish coats with dry time between. Cleat mounting: ~30 minutes. Realistic total: one evening of shop time plus a couple days of finish drying in the background.
